## The 7 Real Reasons Your Photo Gets Rejected

### 1. File Size Too Large
Most government portals are built on older server infrastructure. They cannot handle huge, high-resolution files. Generally, they require your photo to be between **10KB to 50KB** (or max 200KB).

Meanwhile, modern smartphone cameras click photos that are anywhere from **3MB to 8MB**! That's up to 100 times larger than the allowed limit.

> **Pro Tip:** Direct compression is critical. If you use a random online tool, your image might lose so much clarity that the automated verification system will flag it again for being blurry.

### 2. Wrong File Format
The portal strictly asks for a **JPG or JPEG** format. But if you take a photo with a newer iPhone, it saves as HEIC by default. Or maybe you downloaded a screenshot in PNG format. 

Automated validation APIs look at the file's extension and MIME-type. If it's not strictly `.jpg` or `.jpeg`, they reject it instantly.

### 3. Wrong Pixel Dimensions
When a portal says "200x200 pixels," it means *exactly* 200x200! It’s not a suggestion. 

Automated matching systems immediately reject photos that are incorrectly sized. If you try to stretch or squash an image manually, your face looks distorted, and the manual reviewer will instantly reject your form.

### 4. Wrong Aspect Ratio
A standard passport-size photograph has an aspect ratio based on **3.5 cm x 4.5 cm** (rectangular, not square). 

A lot of applicants simply upload a square cropped photo from their smartphone gallery, which gets summarily rejected for failing the dimension ratio checks.

### 5. Background Color is Not Plain White
This is a massive reason for rejection during manual review. Almost all official portals (like UPSC, SSC, NEET) explicitly demand a **plain white or light background**. 

Standing against a curtain, having shadows behind your head, or having details in the background will get your application rejected during the manual review phase.

### 6. Photo is Blurry or Low Quality
Rules state the photo should not be more than 3 months old. More practically, if your photo is blurry or heavily pixelated after compression, the security filters can't verify your identity.

### 7. Unique Rules for Different Portals
Here’s the biggest trap: The photo you used for your Aadhaar card will NOT work for SSC. The signature you scanned for your Passport will NOT work for NEET. Every portal has completely different size, format, and dimension specifications.
