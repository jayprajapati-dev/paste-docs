Along with your photograph, your digital signature is the absolute validation of your registration. Recruitment boards and university registration channels use signature images to counter-authenticate your identity during exams, interviews, and final onboarding processes.

Yet, formatting signature images is notoriously buggy. Standard portals demand files between **10 KB to 20 KB** shaped with a wide aspect ratio like **140 x 60 pixels**.

If you try to crop a signature with basic gallery crops, you're usually left with massive blank white borders or clipped letters, both of which trigger rejections. In this guide, we break down how to safely format your signature.

---

## 1. Capture High-Contrast Signatures

Getting a clean, high-contrast signature will yield flawless results:

* **Brush/Pen Choice:** Work with a thick gel, ink, or felt pen. A standard ultra-fine ballpoint pen creates extremely thin lines that compress poorly. Black gel lines provide high-contrast definitions.
* **Paper quality:** Use completely plain, thick white printer paper. Avoid thin sheets where writing on the reverse side is visible, or notebook pages with lines.
* **Uniform lighting:** Eliminate shadows that block light from your camera sensor. Stand in flat day brightness.

---

## 2. Setting Up Proper Crop Boundaries

When you upload a signature, the portal's system attempts to isolate the inked lines from the paper.

If there are huge blank zones on the left and right, the inked signature will be scaled down to a tiny, illegible line in the center of the screen, failing human verification checks during exams.

### The Perfect Crop Alignment:
* **Vertical padding:** Keep a thin margin of blank white space above the highest point of your letters and below the lowest loop (e.g. loops in `g`, `y`, `j`).
* **Horizontal bounds:** Crop exactly where your signature begins and ends, leaving just a sliver of space on the far-left and far-right.
* **Aspect consistency:** Keep the aspect ratio wide (roughly 7:3 or 2:1).
