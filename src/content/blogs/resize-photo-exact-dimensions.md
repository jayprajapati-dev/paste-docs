When registering online for Central government applications, passport issuing portals, or visa clearances, you will regularly encounter this dimension mandate: **"Upload a photograph with dimensions of precisely 3.5 cm in width and 4.5 cm in height."**

Most students assume that since they are uploading a file online, centimeter measurements don't apply. But to a scanner or image sensor on servers, centimeter instructions correspond to explicit **pixel proportions and aspect ratios**.

In this guide, we will break down the exact math behind "3.5 cm x 4.5 cm" photographs, and show you how to crop your photo to these exact guidelines in under 10 seconds.

---

## 1. Centimeters to Pixels Conversion Math

An online server cannot physically measure a photo with a ruler. Instead, it calculates size using **DPI (Dots Per Inch)** or **PPI (Pixels Per Inch)** standards.

Standard high-quality scans are processed at **300 DPI**. Let's convert centimeter measurements into screen pixels using standard math:

* **Calculation:** 1 inch is equal to **2.54 centimeters**.
* **Width in Inches:** 3.5 cm / 2.54 = **1.38 inches**.
* **Height in Inches:** 4.5 cm / 2.54 = **1.77 inches**.
* **Digital Width (at 300 DPI):** 1.38 inches x 300 = **413 pixels**.
* **Digital Height (at 300 DPI):** 1.77 inches x 300 = **531 pixels**.

So, when a portal demands a **3.5 cm x 4.5 cm** passport photograph, it wants an image with an **exact 7:9 aspect ratio** and a resolution of **413 x 531 pixels**.

## 2. Setting Up Proper Passport Crops

If you manually crop a square photo, you are throwing away vertical space, which cuts off your chin or hair. If you crop too wide, your shoulders are too broad and ruin the symmetry of the photo.

When cropping your passport photograph, try to satisfy this aspect breakdown:
1. **Facial Space:** 70% of the vertical space should showcase your head (from the crown of your hair to the base of your chin).
2. **Shoulder Margin:** The remaining 30% should reveal just the top portion of your collarbone and shoulders.
3. **Level Gaze:** Ensure your nose sits dead-center in the cropped canvas, looking straight ahead.
