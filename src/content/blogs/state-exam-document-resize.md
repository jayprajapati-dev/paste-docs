While central exams like UPSC and NEET occupy major national column inches, **state-level examinations** are the gateway for millions of students. Whether you are registering for Gujarat Technical University (GTU) exams, the Diploma to Degree Common Entrance Test (DDCET), Maharashtra's MHT-CET, or Karnataka's KCET, registering online is the first step of your career.

However, state universities and boards often run on older, localized portal software. These platforms have notoriously strict validation parameters and provide vague error logs like **"Error code #401: Internal format exception"** or **"Incorrect Aspect File."**

In this state-exam documentation manual, we break down how to handle state portal upload constraints cleanly.

---

## 1. Typical State Exam Specifications

Most state portals enforce criteria based on physical paper prints:

* **GTU & DDCET:** Photo sizing is usually restricted to **less than 100KB**, expecting a JPG of **150 x 200 pixels**. Signature limits are extremely tiny—usually under **15KB**.
* **MHT-CET:** Photo requirements are **20KB to 50KB** (**3.5cm x 4.5cm**). Signature requirements are strictly **10KB to 20KB**.
* **KCET:** Photo and Signature under **40KB** each, with dimensions matching **320 x 240 pixels**.

---

## 2. Managing Marks Card and Certificate Sizing

State boards also require you to upload scans of your other documents:
1. **10th & 12th Standard Marksheets** (often required between **100KB to 300KB**).
2. **Income & Caste Certificates** (frequently required in small PDFs or JPGs under **500KB**).
3. **Diploma/Degree Provisional Certificates** (often in PDF formats under **1MB**).

If you take a photo of your physical Marks Card using your phone, the layout is usually distorted, tilted, and spans around 4MB. 

> **Pro Tip:** When photographing paper certificates, stand in direct, flat daylight to ensure even brightness. Never crop on an angle; align the camera parallel to the sheet to ensure high contrast for the digital OCR matching systems.
