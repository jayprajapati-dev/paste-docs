The National Testing Agency (NTA) requires every NEET UG 2026 aspirant to upload **two photographs** as part of the online application: a passport-size photo and a postcard-size photo. Both have strict specifications for dimensions, file size, format and background. If either photo does not meet the spec, the application is rejected — and the correction window is short.

This guide covers the exact NEET 2026 photo size requirements, common rejection reasons, and how to resize your photo to meet the spec in under 30 seconds using a free, privacy-first tool that runs entirely in your browser.

## NEET 2026 Passport-Size Photo Specifications

The passport-size photo is the primary photo that appears on your NEET admit card and is used for identity verification at the exam centre. NTA specifies:

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Dimensions | 3.5 × 4.5 cm (approximately 200 × 230 pixels at 96 DPI) |
| File size | 10 KB to 200 KB |
| Format | JPG / JPEG |
| Background | Plain white |
| Face coverage | At least 80% of the photo area |
| Pose | Front-facing, both ears visible, neutral expression |

The photo must be a recent photograph (taken within the last 6 months). NTA recommends that the candidate's name and the date the photo was taken be printed on the photo itself — this is a critical requirement that many aspirants miss.

## NEET 2026 Postcard-Size Photo Specifications

The postcard-size photo is a **second, separate upload** required by NTA. It is larger than the passport photo and is used for additional identity verification. The specification is:

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Dimensions | 4 × 6 inches (approximately 600 × 900 pixels at 96 DPI) |
| File size | 10 KB to 200 KB |
| Format | JPG / JPEG |
| Background | Plain white |
| Face coverage | At least 80% of the photo area, both ears visible |

Both photos must be uploaded separately during the NEET application. You cannot use the same photo for both — the dimensions are different.

## Common NEET Photo Rejection Reasons

Based on analysis of NEET application data, the five most common photo rejection reasons are:

1. **File size outside the 10-200KB range** — the most common rejection. Use the compress tools above to land within the range.
2. **Wrong dimensions** — the passport photo must be 3.5×4.5 cm and the postcard photo must be 4×6 inch. Use the dedicated tools to apply the exact dimensions.
3. **Face coverage below 80%** — the photo is too zoomed out. Crop tighter around the face and upper shoulders.
4. **Ears not visible** — hair covering the ears, or photo taken at an angle. Retake the photo facing the camera directly with hair pulled back.
5. **Name and date not printed on the photo** — NTA requires this. Use the Add Name & Date tool.

## NEET Signature Specifications

In addition to the two photos, NEET requires a scanned signature upload:

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Dimensions | Approximately 140 × 60 pixels |
| File size | 10 KB to 30 KB |
| Format | JPG / JPEG |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use the same photo for the passport-size and postcard-size upload?**
No. The dimensions are different (3.5×4.5 cm vs 4×6 inch). You need two separate photos, both meeting the 10-200KB file size range.

**Does NEET require a postcard photo in 2026?**
Yes. NTA has required both a passport-size photo and a postcard-size photo since 2024. The postcard photo specification is 4×6 inches, 10-200KB, JPG format.

**What happens if my NEET photo is rejected?**
NTA opens a correction window after the application deadline. During this window, you can re-upload corrected photos. However, the correction window is typically only 2-3 days, so it is better to get the photo right the first time.

**Can I smile in my NEET photo?**
NTA recommends a neutral expression. A slight smile is usually accepted, but avoid wide smiles that change the facial proportions. The key requirement is that both ears are visible and the face is front-facing.

**Is my photo uploaded to a server when I use PasteDocs?**
No. PasteDocs runs entirely in your browser using the Canvas API. Your photo is processed in your device's local memory and never transmitted to any server.
